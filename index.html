The 23.5-Degree Tilt

Structural Explanation of the Excerpt from The End of Time

Foundational Verse

“10 And he said [to Cain], What hast thou done? the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground. And now art thou cursed from the earth, which hath opened her mouth to receive thy brother's blood from thy hand; When thou tillest the ground, it shall not henceforth yield unto thee her strength; a fugitive and a vagabond shalt thou be in the earth.” — Genesis 4:10-12

This verse establishes the core principle behind the excerpt: the idea that moral actions produce physical consequences in the world itself. The ground becomes a witness, and the planet becomes part of the judgment. The excerpt builds directly on this structural concept.

Purpose of This Excerpt

This excerpt is a structural interpretation of Earth's axial tilt. It connects the biblical curse on Cain with the physical consequences of the planet’s 23.5-degree tilt. The argument is not theological commentary or scientific speculation; it is a unified explanation of how a moral judgment is expressed through planetary mechanics.

Core Structural Claims

The excerpt makes three primary claims:

  1. The tilt expresses the curse on the ground.
    The 23.5-degree tilt creates winter, seasonal death, scarcity, and perpetual labor. This matches the biblical statement that the ground would no longer yield freely.
  2. The tilt creates wandering.
    Cain was condemned to wander, and the Earth itself now “wanders” through space due to axial precession — a slow wobble caused by the tilt. The planet mirrors Cain’s condition.
  3. The night sky encodes the same structure.
    Because of the tilt, the North Pole points toward a configuration involving Ursa Major (The Plow), Ursa Minor (The Anchor), and Draco (The Dragon). These symbols align with labor, direction, and the dragon is defined as "satan" (Revelation 12:9).

Why This Matters

The excerpt shows how a single physical parameter — Earth's axial tilt — produces a complete system of consequences: seasons, agricultural cycles, planetary wobble, and symbolic sky patterns. These consequences match the narrative structure of Cain's exile, forming a unified interpretation of labor, wandering, and cosmic order.

Ursa Major is universally known as “The Plow,” which fits the ancient symbolism of the destroyer: a blade that tears open the earth. This aligns with Jesus’ statement in the King James Version: “No man, having put his hand to the plow, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God.” (Luke 9:62). The excerpt interprets this structurally: returning to the plow represents returning to the old system of labor, destruction, and the curse placed on the ground.

The excerpt also explains that if the Earth’s axis were perpendicular to the Sun, the North Pole would point toward Lyra. In that configuration, every day would resemble the first day of spring: equal day and night, balanced temperatures, and continuous growth. Plants, trees, and flowers would flourish year-round. This matches the description of the new creation in Revelation, where the Tree of Life yields fruit every month throughout the year (Revelation 22:2). The upright axis corresponds structurally to an unfallen world.
